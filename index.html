Abstract

Robots and other physical-AI systems increasingly rely on vision-language models (VLMs) to perceive a scene and predict how it will evolve. Yet many questions in video benchmarks can be answered from a single frame, so a high score does not show that a model followed the scene over time. We introduce GRIP-V, the video version of GRIP: 40 procedurally generated sub-benchmarks in seven reasoning families, from mechanics and fluids to occlusion, agents, and rule tracking, with 2,500 clips per sub-benchmark and ten questions per clip (1 million questions). Every answer is computed from the simulated scene state and checked by a validator. Questions climb a five-rung ladder from observation through relations, measurement, and multi-step integration to counterfactual reasoning, and 81.1% are flagged as requiring motion. We evaluated eleven open-weight VLMs on a 100,000-question subset. The best model answers 55.6% of the questions, against a constant-answer baseline of 28.1%. Shown only the final frame, three of five tested models lose 14–15 points of baseline-adjusted accuracy, so they do use motion. Accuracy, however, falls by up to 37 points from the first rung to the last, eight of eleven models are within 6 points of the baseline on counterfactuals, and no model reliably detects injected rule violations. Current VLMs can follow how a scene unfolds, but not yet predict how it would change under an intervention or notice when it breaks the rules.

How GRIP-V is built

Ground truth from the simulator, not from annotators

Each sub-benchmark is a seeded generator. The scene is simulated first; the video and the answers are both derived from the same scene state, and a separate validator re-derives every answer.

Simulate

A seeded generator samples one of 185 scenario variants and simulates the scene state over time: collisions, flows, flashes, moves.

Render

Every frame is drawn from that state at 1280×720 and 24 fps, for at most 10 s. On-screen text is limited to a clock and needed scale labels.

Ask

Ten questions per clip on the five-rung ladder, with answers computed from the state. 81.1% require motion: event counts, order, timing.

Validate

A validator rebuilds all 100,000 clips and re-derives all 1M answers: zero mismatches. It flags all 120 answers corrupted on purpose.
